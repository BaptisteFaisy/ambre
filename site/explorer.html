In your browser — the project's own explorer, compiled to WebAssembly

Cut the power anywhere

This page runs the Ambre power-cut explorer inside your browser: the same C sources as ambre-explore (fmt/, host/explore/, host/simdisk/), compiled to WebAssembly — no re-implementation, no server, no dependency. You choose the instant the 12 V rail dies and how it dies; the real recovery code runs on the real journal image, and the eight invariants are checked against what was really injected.

The verdict

The recovery runs, then the invariant checker compares the result with the injected ground truth. Events acquired before the cut must come back byte-identical; nothing may be invented; a locked event must stay locked; the "complete file" flag must tell the truth; and running the recovery twice must give the same table.

    Reproduce it: make all && node scripts/web-conformance.mjs --states 0 --nested. The machine-checked result is committed in docs/results/web-emulator-conformance.json; it compares every counter and fails on a single difference.

    The module loaded by this page: site/assets/ambre-emu.js, 154 615 bytes, sha256 —. It is built by scripts/build-web.sh (Emscripten) from the sources of the commit that published it; the module verifies its own CRC-32C against the normative check value 0xE3069283 before it shows anything.

    What this page does not prove. The journal runs on the simulated disk of the PC host, not on QNX 8.0 and not on real media: the timing figures of the validation page come from the PC campaigns; the QNX target itself runs the chain (see the target page), but its latency figures are BLOCKED(not measured on target yet). A browser tab is also not a real-time environment — the wall-clock numbers above are only a cost of computing, never a latency claim.

    The campaign of record remains the PC one: 1 379 264 power-cut states over 18 scenarios, 0 violations of I1..I8 — see the validation page for every figure and the command that reproduces it.
